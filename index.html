I What Oz brings

Everything it adds

It's still the game you own, on id's own engine, with id's rules. Oz changes how it looks, sounds and plays on a PC today.

Every texture remade

All of id's images upscaled four times, id's own colors kept, with normal, roughness and specular maps. Up close, walls keep their detail instead of turning to blocks.

35 arenas relit

Every map lit again the way id's compiler lit it, now with directional light maps, so the new surfaces catch the light. Softer shadows, light that bounces, and the same brightness id chose.

29 rigged fighters

Every character rebuilt as a skinned, skeletal model with smooth animation, shown on every server and mod, CPMA and Defrag included.

Ragdolls

Bodies fall with Jolt Physics and muscle tone in the joints. A rail throws them with the hit, and moving platforms carry them.

Remade effects

Glowing rail trails, layered lightning, sparks, smoke and scorch marks. Pick your own lightning and plasma colors, or switch back to id's effects.

3D sound

OpenAL Soft with HRTF, so you hear where a shot came from, and hit sounds pitched by how hard you hit.

Same at every frame rate

In matches running Oz, movement steps every 8 ms, so jumps, footsteps and fire rates are the same at 60 fps as at 1000.

Every community server

One browser for vanilla, CPMA and Defrag servers. Join switches to the server's mod by itself and back again when you leave. Pure servers always let you in.

Lag compensation

In matches running Oz, every instant-hit shot lands where you saw your target, whatever your ping, and your own shots show the moment you fire. Neil Toronto's Unlagged, fitted to Oz.

One HUD everywhere

Oz's HUD, scoreboard and results on every server and every mod, pure servers included, with four presets or every part set by hand.

A map editor, built in

Brushes, curves and entities in one view or four. Open any map you own, then compile and play it without leaving the game. Maps for Defrag and CPMA too.

A career of your own

XP and levels, achievements, records and a player card built from what you've earned, across a campaign, training, skirmish and online play.

II The look

The arenas, as you remember them

From across a room Oz keeps id's look on purpose: the same colors, the same brightness, the same arenas. The difference shows up close, where id's textures turn to blurred blocks and Oz's keep their detail.

  • Every texture remade: the world, the fighters, the weapons, the items and the effects, each with its own normal, roughness and specular maps. Every surface stays matte.
  • 35 arenas relit with q3map2 as id's own compiler lit them, now with directional light maps and light that bounces.
  • A modern renderer: ioquake3's physically based rend2, with tone mapping, bloom and AMD FSR 1 upscaling.
  • New level shots and cinematics: the arenas' pictures shot in the new look, and the original movies in higher quality.
  • Id's look whenever you want it: pick the OpenGL or Vulkan renderer and nothing Oz adds loads.

III Fighters, effects and sound

Flesh, bone and fire

  • 29 rigged fighters with smooth skeletal animation in place of the original's vertex-animated models, on every server and mod.
  • Ragdolls on Jolt Physics, with muscle tone in the joints. The map's brushes and moving platforms are solid to them.
  • Remade effects: glowing rail trails, layered lightning, sparks, smoke and scorch marks, with your own lightning and plasma colors.
  • 3D sound through OpenAL Soft with HRTF, and a choice of hit sounds pitched by damage.
  • Your gun, your way: the gun editor moves, turns and scales the weapon in your view, with presets and widescreen placement.

IV Playing online

Every server, one client

  • The Server Browser asks every master server and lists the vanilla, CPMA and Defrag servers that answer, with search, filters, favorites, history and LAN.
  • Mods switch by themselves: join a CPMA or Defrag server and Oz switches to it, then back again when you leave. Their own code decides movement and rules.
  • Pure servers stay playable. A server that forces its own files always lets you in. Oz leaves its packs out there and keeps the lighting, the menus and the HUD.
  • The server's rules: your game predicts with the server's own settings, so a server on id's physics feels right.
  • Lag compensation in matches running Oz: instant-hit shots land where you saw your target.
  • With friends: friends, parties, Quick Play and peer-to-peer matches hosted from your own PC. Hide my IP sends a match through a relay.
  • Joining without the map: Oz fetches a missing map from the server or the map archives as you join.

V Playing alone

The arena never sleeps

  • Campaign: the original's tiers of arenas, up to the final duel.
  • Skirmish and Custom Match: bots on any arena, in any mode, with your rules and modifiers, under Quake III Arena's own rules, CPMA or Defrag.
  • Defrag races on any map you own, with search and Oz's estimate of each course's difficulty.
  • Training for movement, aim and duels.
  • Your career: XP and levels, achievements, records and a player card you build from what you've earned.
  • Replays: every match recorded, watched from any player's view, seeking both ways.

VI The HUD

One HUD on every server

  • Drawn by the engine itself, so it's the same on every server and every mod, pure servers included.
  • Presets: Modern, Classic, Competitive and Minimal, or set each part yourself.
  • A scoreboard and results with each player's weapons, accuracy and damage, for up to 64 players. Right-click under Tab to open a player's card and add them as a friend.
  • The crosshair editor: shapes, size, color and outline, previewed exactly as the match draws them.
  • A speedometer, chat with emoji, and item timers for spectators.

VII Menus and settings

Every screen is Oz's

  • New menus throughout: the main menu, pause menu, connection screen and error dialogs replace id's and the mods' screens. Mouse, keyboard and controller all work.
  • Settings for video, graphics, audio, controls, mouse, crosshair, HUD, gameplay, network and accessibility.
  • Accessibility: menu and text size, high contrast and reduced flashing.
  • Bring your old config: your Quake III binds and settings come over on first run, if you want them.
  • A Mod Library for the mods you have installed.

VIII The map editor

Build your own arena

  • Brushes, curves and entities with select, brush, clip and faces tools, hollow, subtract, merge and texture lock.
  • One view or four: 3D, top, front and side.
  • Materials and entities from your own install and its map packs, with search and favorites.
  • Open any map you own: stock maps open ready to change.
  • Compile and play without leaving: Play drops you in, Esc brings you back.
  • Maps for Defrag and CPMA too, saved as standard .map files.

IX Level design

Build arenas worth fighting in

The level design guide is about what makes a Quake III Arena duel map play well for years: using height, keeping players moving, connecting rooms, placing armor and weapons, setting spawns and shaping sound. It's built on the articles the competitive mapping community learned from, with the game's real numbers and a diagram for every idea.

  • Flow, connectivity and balance: why maps loop, why a room needs more than one door, and how a layout pulls players before any item goes in.
  • Items and spawns: armor sets and timing, weapon placement, health totals, and exactly how Quake III Arena picks where you come back.
  • The numbers: jump heights, ledges, doorways, respawn times and weapon reach, measured in the game.
  • A checklist to work through before you release.

X Getting in

Four steps to the arena

Oz reads the game from your own copy and never ships or changes id's files. You need Quake III Arena: buy it on Steam if you don't have it.

  1. 01

    Download Setup

    A 1 MB installer for Windows 10 or 11.

    OzArenaSetup.exe
  2. 02

    Let it find the game

    Setup finds Quake III Arena in your Steam libraries, among your GOG games, or in the usual folders. If it doesn't, press Locate.

  3. 03

    Install

    About 4 GB, into a folder of its own inside your game's, so the two never mix. A stopped download carries on where it left off.

  4. 04

    Play

    From the Start Menu or your desktop. Run Setup again to update; it skips the world packs you already have.

Windows

Setup above, or by hand: the release's zip and its two world packs.

Linux

XI Under the hood

id Tech 3, rebuilt

Oz runs on a modified build of the game's own engine, which id released under the GPL.

Quake3e

The engine's core: fast, current, and kept in step with upstream.

ioquake3

The rend2 renderer and OpenAL sound, extended with Oz's look.

oDFe and CNQ3

Defrag's tools and CPMA's extensions, so both mods play at their best.

Three renderers

oz for the new look, opengl and vulkan for id's. Menus and HUD draw through all three.

Jolt Physics

The ragdolls, with the map's brushes and moving platforms as colliders.

RmlUi

The native menus and the HUD, drawn from the network snapshot under any mod.
